## 1. Market size, segments, digital record-keeping adoption and tools in use

### Takeaway
Kenya's MSME base is large and mostly informal. The most recent official census-type count is still the 2016 KNBS survey: about 7.4M MSMEs, of which only about 1.56M are licensed. As of mid-2026 only about 0.75M taxpayers are on eTIMS, and only about 16% of MSMEs reach formal credit. Digital bookkeeping is a minority behaviour. Most small firms run on cash, M-Pesa and informal records, and they struggle less with capturing data than with turning it into usable insight.

### Cited Findings
- **Total MSMEs (2016 data, still the official baseline):** the KNBS 2016 MSME Survey estimated about 1.56M MSMEs licensed by the 47 county governments plus 5.85M unlicensed (largely household-based) businesses, about 7.41M in total. Sample: 50,043 licensed MSMEs plus 14,000 households. — [KNBS 2016 MSME Survey Basic Report](https://www.knbs.or.ke/2016-micro-small-and-medium-enterprises-msme-survey-basic-report-2/); [KNBS NADA catalog 69](https://statistics.knbs.or.ke/nada/index.php/catalog/69)
- MSME counts in Kenya are contested between sources (Africa Check called the data a "tower of Babel"), so any TAM should carry a range. — [Africa Check](https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/reports/fact-checking-president-kenyatta-kenyas-small-businesses-its-data-tower-babel)
- **Business churn (2022-2023):** the CBK/KNBS/FSD Kenya MSE Tracker Survey Wave II (June 2023) re-contacted FinAccess 2019/2021 respondents. Of 2,394 respondents, 1,715 businesses were still operating and 679 (about 28%) had closed. — [KNBS NADA catalog 129 (MSE Tracker Survey)](https://statistics.knbs.or.ke/nada/index.php/catalog/129)
- **Formal credit reach:** only about 1.1M of about 7.4M MSMEs (about 16%) access formal credit. The formal MSME financing market is valued at USD 45.4bn. — [The Star, 7 Jun 2024](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/kenya/2024-06-07-kenyas-small-ventures-lack-formal-credit-access--survey)
- **Formal-tax footprint (a proxy for the "formal SME" segment):** 750,915 taxpayers had onboarded to eTIMS as of 30 June 2026 (KRA FY2025/26 outturn). — [People Daily, KRA FY2025/26 record revenue](https://peopledaily.digital/business/kra-posts-record-ksh2-84t-revenue-collection-in-fy2025-26); [Huduma Global summary](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/kra-revenue-2026-etims-sep-diaspora-kenyans). A search extract also cites about 680,000 onboarded during calendar 2025; I could not confirm which outlet published it.
- **Small Firm Diaries (FSD Kenya/NYU FAI, 2022-23, 130+ firms in Nairobi, Kisumu and Kwale, firms with 1-20 employees):**
  - Revenue and expenses swing unpredictably month to month.
  - Owners mix business and personal finances and take multiple loans to cover both.
  - Despite Kenya's high financial inclusion, small firms still operate "mostly in cash or through informal channels like chamas".
  - Working capital is the main unmet need.
  - Press coverage reported that the firms net under Sh240,000 a month.
  — [FSD Kenya SFD summary](https://www.fsdkenya.org/blogs-publications/a-new-perspective-on-small-firms-in-kenya-summary-of-key-findings-from-the-small-firm-diaries); [FSD SFD draft report (Apr 2023)](https://www.fsdkenya.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/23-04-11-SFD-Draft-Report.pdf); [People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/business/small-businesses-net-less-sh240000-monthly-study)
- **Record-keeping behaviour (IGC, 2026):**
  - Nearly 40% of SMEs in sub-Saharan Africa record information infrequently or not at all.
  - The main barrier is extracting useful insight from data, not collecting it.
  - In a Nairobi randomised trial, a smartphone POS app with automated analytics raised daily record-keeping by 7-9 percentage points, with the biggest effects among resource-constrained owners.
  — [IGC, "Data without insight" (Kenya)](https://www.theigc.org/publications/data-without-insight-why-small-businesses-under-invest-record-keeping-evidence-kenya); [Ashraf & Lyons policy brief, May 2026](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/Ashraf-Lyons-Policy-Brief-May-2026.pdf)
- **Accounting software use (older academic data, small sample):** a Nairobi urban study found 72% of SMEs surveyed used no accounting application. In another sample only 21 of 73 respondents used computerised accounting. Cost and system functionality were the main uptake factors. — [Academia.edu, Mobile Accounting Systems awareness, Nairobi](https://www.academia.edu/65424557/Awareness_of_the_Mobile_Accounting_Systems_for_Kenyan_Small_to_Medium_Enterprises_Smes_Case_of_Nairobi_Urban)
- **Tools seen in market:**
  - Zoho Books and TallyPrime are cited as the common SME eTIMS-compliant packages in 2026. — [Ai SaaS Writer 2026 guide](https://aisaaswriter.com/common-account-audit-software-used-in-kenya/) (low-authority blog)
  - Sage ERPs (X3, 300, Intacct) get eTIMS through partner add-ons such as Greytrix. — [Greytrix Africa](https://www.greytrix.com/africa/product/other-solutions/e-invoicing-solutions/e-invoicing-for-sage-erp-kenya/)
  - Owners use the Safaricom M-PESA for Business app for real-time and exportable statements, money-in/money-out charts and multi-till views (launched about 2020). — [Khusoko 2020](https://khusoko.com/2020/06/18/safaricom-mpesa-for-business-app-allows-smes-to-track-performance-digitally/?amp=1); [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/lipa-na-m-pesa-safaricom-business-app)
- A Kenyan headline describes "5.8 million Kenyan MSMEs" as still running on spreadsheets. This is a low-quality aggregator, cited only as an indicator of narrative. — [Streamlinefeed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/why-kenyan-msmes-still-run-on-spreadsheets)

### Inferences
- **Realistic segments for Ledger Link:**
  - **Formal and compliance-exposed:** on the order of 0.75M eTIMS-onboarded taxpayers. This includes individuals with business income, so the count of companies and SMEs is lower.
  - **Licensed but lightly formal:** about 1.5M+ county-licensed MSMEs.
  - **Long informal tail:** about 5.9M, reached only through ultra-light, free, eTIMS-Lite-style tools.
- The beachhead is the formal and licensed segments plus the accountants who serve them.
- The IGC evidence suggests owners will keep records if the app gives back insight automatically: cash position, margin, "you can afford payroll". A plain ledger-entry screen is not enough. The owner-facing dashboard is a retention driver, not decoration.
- High churn (about 28% closure in the tracker panel) means monthly SMB subscription churn will be structurally high. Accountant-held, multi-client accounts are likely stickier.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 KNBS MSME census. The 2016 survey remains the latest; a new KNBS MSME survey may exist but was not found.
- I could not retrieve FinAccess 2024 MSME-specific or record-keeping figures, MSE Tracker figures on technology use, or GSMA/IFC/World Bank data on the share of Kenyan MSMEs using accounting software. No robust national percentage for digital bookkeeping adoption was found.
- The widely repeated claim that "46% of MSMEs close within the first year" (KNBS 2016-derived) appeared only in a low-quality source. It is unverified.

## 2. Compliance pressures driving digitisation (eTIMS, deductibility, enforcement, TCC/AGPO) and SME reactions

### Takeaway
eTIMS has moved from an invoicing mandate to the backbone of income-tax validation:
- From 1 Jan 2024, expenses are deductible only with an eTIMS invoice.
- From 1 Jan 2026, KRA validates income and expenses in returns for the 2025 year against eTIMS, withholding tax and customs data.
- The Finance Act 2026 adds minimum penalties of KES 100,000 for companies and KES 10,000 for individuals.

Business reaction is grudging compliance plus loud complaints about supplier non-compliance, cost, low awareness and repeated KRA outages. These are pain points a third-party tool can solve.

### Cited Findings
- **Deductibility rule:** Finance Act 2023 s.12 amended Income Tax Act s.16(1). From 1 Jan 2024, no deduction is allowed for expenditure not supported by an eTIMS-generated invoice. — [CM Advocates](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/demystifying-etims-why-every-business-in-kenya-should-pay-attention/); [KAM eTIMS update](https://kam.co.ke/?p=7252)
- **Non-VAT taxpayers:** non-VAT-registered taxpayers were also required to onboard to eTIMS. — [EY tax alert](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-requires-non-vat-registered-taxpayers-to-onboard-on-e-tims)
- **Return validation from 1 Jan 2026:**
  - KRA validates income and expenses declared in individual and non-individual 2025 returns against TIMS/eTIMS invoices, withholding tax certificates and customs import records.
  - Exclusions: emoluments, imports, investment allowances, interest income, airline passenger tickets, and payments subject to final withholding tax.
  - iTax may block or flag mismatched returns, exposing filers to late-filing penalties.
  - Advisers recommend pre-filing reconciliations and making sure every vendor issues eTIMS invoices.
  — [EY Tax News 2025-2471](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2471-kenya-revenue-authority-to-validate-income-and-expenses-in-income-tax-returns); [Tarra Agility alert](https://tarraagility.com/kra-to-validate-income-and-expenses-in-2025-income-tax-returns/); [MMW Advocates](https://mmw.legal/kra-is-validating-income-and-expenses/); [KRA public notice: filing 2025 returns](https://www.kra.go.ke/news-center/public-notices/2375-filing-of-2025-income-tax-returns)
- **Finance Act 2026 penalties:** failure to use eTIMS now carries a minimum penalty of KES 100,000 for companies and KES 10,000 for individuals. — [Business Daily, eTIMS outage article](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/outage-on-kra-s-etims-systems-5536700)
  - During debate on the Finance Bill 2026, concerns were raised over a proposed KES 100,000 fine, or twice the tax due, for failing to issue an electronic receipt. — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/finance-bill-2026-concerns-raised-over-ksh100k-fines-for-tax-non-compliance-n382651)

  - integration of iTax, iCMS and IFMIS

### Inferences
- The highest-value compliance job is no longer "issue an eTIMS invoice". KRA, ERP partners and Zoho already do that. It is making sure every expense and income line will match KRA's data before filing. Product implications:
  - a purchase-side eTIMS matcher that flags bills without a valid eTIMS invoice or supplier PIN
  - a "deductibility at risk" KES total
  - a pre-filing reconciliation report against KRA's validation logic, respecting the exclusions list
  - a supplier-chasing workflow (WhatsApp or SMS request for an eTIMS invoice)
- KRA outages plus heavy penalties create demand for offline queueing and retry of eTIMS submissions, with an audit trail showing that an attempt was made during an outage.
- TCC and AGPO create a concrete owner job: "keep me tender-eligible". A compliance-status widget showing filing status, TCC expiry and outstanding liabilities is a strong trust feature for owners bidding for government work.
- Expansion into East Africa requires one e-invoicing integration per country: Uganda EFRIS, Rwanda EBM, Tanzania VFD/EFD. The Kenya eTIMS work is a template, not a reusable integration.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the KES 2M penalty proposal's year or outcome, or whether KRA's SME alternative to eTIMS launched.
- No data was found on what share of eTIMS invoices come from third-party integrators vs KRA's free tools, or on churn from eTIMS.
- I found nothing current on Tanzania's VFD/EFD SME experience.
- The ICPAK position on eTIMS deductibility was not found.

## 3. Top pain points of Kenyan SMEs and bookkeepers

### Takeaway
The dominant pains are:
- cash-flow volatility
- late payment, especially from government: KES 465.9bn in pending bills
- no fundable books, which locks SMEs out of credit
- mixed personal and business money
- a growing stack of statutory deductions changed in 2024-2026: SHIF, Affordable Housing Levy, NSSF Year 4
- eTIMS supplier compliance
- unreliable power and KRA systems

Software priced in USD, carrying 16% VAT on foreign digital services, adds cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Credit access:**
  - The CBK 2024 Survey Report on MSME Access to Bank Credit is reported to identify inadequate record-keeping and collateral as the primary barriers to formal credit. Term loans and overdrafts make up over 85% of MSME lending. (Seen via secondary sources; the primary CBK report was not retrieved.) — [Streamlinefeed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/why-kenyan-msmes-still-run-on-spreadsheets); [KBA working paper WPS10/26, Mar 2026](https://www.kba.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/WPS1026-Wairimu-Kiragu.pdf)
  - Business Daily: "SMEs pay high penalty for poor record keeping". — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/smes-pay-high-penalty-for-poor-record-keeping--2003494)
  - Banks direct the bulk of MSME loan accounts (86% in 2022) to medium enterprises, so micro and small firms are underserved. — [The Star, Jun 2024](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/kenya/2024-06-07-kenyas-small-ventures-lack-formal-credit-access--survey)
  - Alternative-data lending research on Kenya exists. — [FinRegLab 2024](https://finreglab.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/FinRegLab_2024-03-28_Research-Report_Alternative-Data-and-Market-Dynamics.pdf)
  - A separate commentary sizes the MSME financing gap at Sh2.5tn. — [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/the-sh2-5-trillion-financing-gap-holding-back-kenyan-businesses/)
- **Late payment:**
  - Public pending bills were KES 465.87bn in March 2026, up 10.5% from KES 421.6bn in March 2025.
  - Suppliers wait 30-90 days and often more than a year.
  - SMEs are turning to structured trade finance.
  - Treasury set aside KES 68bn in FY2026/27, prioritising claims up to KES 100M.
  — [The Star, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/2026-07-27-kenyas-sh465-billion-pending-bills-push-cash-strapped-smes-to-alternative-trade-finance); [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/pending-state-bills-push-smes-to-alternative-financing/); [People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/insights/why-settling-pending-bills-could-be-the-biggest-economic-boost-hidden-in-kenyas-2026-27-budget/amp)
  - Conflicting figure: a lobby group cited more than KES 600bn in April 2025, likely a broader definition. — [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202504170054.html)
- **Volatility and commingling:** small firms show volatile month-to-month revenue and costs, mix personal and business finances, borrow multiple loans for both, and operate largely in cash or chamas. — [FSD Kenya SFD summary](https://www.fsdkenya.org/blogs-publications/a-new-perspective-on-small-firms-in-kenya-summary-of-key-findings-from-the-small-firm-diaries)
- **Payroll statutory stack, 2026 (vendor and blog sources; verify against NSSF and KRA):**
  - **NSSF Year 4 (Feb 2026):**
    - Tier I covers the first KES 9,000.
    - Tier II covers KES 9,001 to 108,000.
    - Contributions are 6% from the employee plus 6% from the employer, capped at KES 6,480 each.
  - **SHIF:** 2.75% of gross pay, minimum KES 300, no cap.
  - **Affordable Housing Levy:** 1.5% from the employee plus 1.5% from the employer.
  - **Ordering:** NSSF, SHIF and AHL are deducted before PAYE.
  - **Deadline:** all are due by the 9th of the following month.
  - **HR licensing:** HR practitioners were to be licensed by IHRM by 31 July 2026.
  — [Faidi HR, NSSF 2026](https://faidihr.com/blog/nssf-rates-2026-kenya-calculator-everything-you-need-to-know); [Faidi HR, payroll explainer](https://faidihr.com/blog/how-payroll-works-in-kenya-paye-nssf-and-shif-explained); [Afrotools](https://afrotools.com/blog/kenya-nssf-contributions-2026/); [CountryTaxCalc](https://www.countrytaxcalc.com/tax-guides/kenya-paye-tax-guide-2026/)
- **eTIMS supplier compliance:** buyers lose deductions when informal, remote or market suppliers cannot issue eTIMS invoices. — [KAM](https://kam.co.ke/?p=7252); [Capital FM, Jan 2026](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2026/01/expert-want-expense-deductibility-delinked-from-etims/)
- **Power:**
  - Consumers averaged 8.39 hours of outages a month against a 1.5-hour regulatory target.
  - System losses were 22.07% against a 16.5% allowance.
  - Nationwide blackouts hit on 4 Dec 2025 and again in 2026; KPLC confirmed "another nationwide blackout" around 1-2 Sept 2026.
  — [People Daily, power crisis](https://peopledaily.digital/business/inside-kenyas-power-crisis-as-outages-rise-and-losses-grow/amp); [Nation](https://www.nation.africa/kenya/business/after-the-blackout-businesses-count-the-cost--5542004); [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/breaking-news/kplc-confirms-another-nationwide-blackout/)

### Inferences
- **Owner jobs to be done:**
  - "Know my real cash today", across M-Pesa till, paybill, bank and cash.
  - "Who owes me and for how long", with government debtors aged separately.
  - "Can I make payroll on the 9th?"
  - "Separate my personal draws from business", for example an owner-drawings categoriser for personal M-Pesa transfers.
  - "Give the bank or lender a credible statement", as a lender-ready pack.
- **Accountant jobs to be done:**
  - bulk M-Pesa statement import and auto-match (CSV and PDF, Daraja API for tills and paybills)
  - payroll that tracks the 2024-26 statutory changes, including P10 and NSSF/SHIF/AHL schedules
  - eTIMS purchase matching and pre-filing validation
  - multi-client month-end
- Late-paying government debtors suggest invoice-financing or receivables partnerships, where Ledger Link's data feeds lenders. That is a channel and monetisation idea, not a core feature.
- Power and connectivity outages argue for offline-tolerant data entry and graceful sync.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative survey of Kenyan bookkeepers' time spent on M-Pesa reconciliation, and no data on how many SMEs mix personal and business M-Pesa lines.
- The primary CBK 2024 MSME credit survey was not retrieved. Its specific percentages, such as the share of rejections due to missing records, are unknown.
- No Kenya-specific data was found on SMEs' failed card payments for USD subscriptions.

## 5. Device, connectivity and language realities

### Takeaway
Kenya is an Android-first (about 92%), mobile-money-saturated (98% penetration) market. Smartphones (48.7M) now clearly outnumber feature phones (29.6M), but about 30M feature phones remain. Unique internet users are only about 23.4M (40.5%). Data is moderately expensive at about USD 0.84-2.25 per GB. Power cuts average more than 8 hours a month, and WhatsApp is a primary channel. The product therefore needs to be a lightweight Android or PWA experience with offline tolerance and WhatsApp/SMS touchpoints.

### Cited Findings
- **Communications Authority of Kenya statistics (period ending Dec 2025; outlets label the quarter variously as Q2 or Q3 FY2025/26):**
  - Smartphones grew 9.1% to 48.7M while feature phones fell 2.6% to 29.6M. Smartphones were 63.7% of connected devices.
  - Mobile money subscriptions rose 5.6% to 51.4M, a 98% penetration rate, and mobile money agents grew 4.4%.
  - Active mobile subscriptions reached 84.1M, a 157.7% penetration rate.

- **Mobile OS share:** Android had 92.4% and iOS 7.29% of mobile OS share in Kenya in July 2026. — [StatCounter Kenya](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/kenya)

### Inferences
- Design for low- to mid-range Android in Chrome. Practical rules:
  - Keep the JS bundle small and lazy-load reports.
  - Cache the last-known dashboard offline.
  - Queue entries and eTIMS submissions while offline.
  - Use compressed sync.
- A PWA is likely acceptable if installable and fast. A Play Store presence still matters for discovery and trust because users search the Play Store, though no direct evidence was found.
- Owner touchpoints should run over WhatsApp and SMS:
  - invoice sharing with an M-Pesa paybill or STK link
  - approval requests
  - daily cash summary
  - payroll-due reminders before the 9th
- Accountants will use desktop or laptop for month-end. The owner works mostly on phone; the accountant is hybrid.
- Feature-phone users (about 30M devices) are reachable only through SMS or USSD. This is relevant for supplier eTIMS chasing and for micro-merchants, not for core Ledger Link users.

### Gaps
- No source was found on Kenyan SME owners' preference for a Swahili vs English UI, or on Sheng use in finance apps. Nothing evidences whether a Swahili UI drives adoption. Treat it as a hypothesis to test, for example with A/B tests on onboarding.
- I found no Kenya-specific data on low-RAM device share, PWA vs native expectations, or Play Store review behaviour for finance apps.
- GSMA Mobile Economy SSA 2025 Kenya-specific figures were not retrieved.
